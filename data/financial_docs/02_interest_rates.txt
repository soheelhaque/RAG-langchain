SYNTHETIC FINANCIAL RESEARCH NOTE
Title: Interest Rates, Inflation, and Equity Discount Rates
Date: 2026-06-30
Source: Northstar Research, fictional training corpus

Policy setting
The Federal Reserve held its policy range at 4.25 to 4.50 percent at the June meeting. Officials
described inflation progress as uneven: goods disinflation continued, while housing and selected
services categories remained firm. The base case is two quarter-point reductions over the next twelve
months, contingent on softer wage growth and inflation moving closer to the 2 percent target. This is
a slower easing path than markets anticipated at the beginning of the year.

The yield curve
The two-year government bond yield ended June at 4.10 percent and the ten-year yield at 4.45 percent.
The positive ten-year minus two-year spread reflected a term premium rather than a strong expectation
of near-term policy tightening. Investors demanded compensation for persistent fiscal borrowing,
uncertain inflation outcomes, and the supply of longer-dated government bonds. A higher term premium
raises the discount rate applied to long-duration cash flows even if the policy rate is unchanged.

Inflation pathways
The central case assumes core inflation slows from 3.0 percent to 2.5 percent by mid-2027. The upside
inflation risk is that service-sector wages and energy costs remain elevated. In that case, the
ten-year yield could rise toward 4.8 percent and the central bank could delay cuts. A downside growth
shock would lower yields more quickly, but it would also reduce revenue expectations for cyclical
industries and credit-sensitive consumers.

Implications for equities
Technology and other growth sectors usually have longer-duration cash flows. Their price-to-earnings
multiples are more sensitive to a rise in real yields than mature value sectors. A semiconductor company
with accelerating earnings can outperform while yields rise, but the hurdle for positive earnings
surprises becomes higher. Banks may benefit from a steepening curve if credit losses stay contained.

AI capital expenditure interaction
AI infrastructure spending has made the rate debate important for large cloud providers. These firms
fund data centres, accelerators, networking, and power contracts before the full revenue stream
arrives. When yields are high, investors scrutinise the payback period, depreciation expense, and
free-cash-flow impact of capital expenditure. If enterprise demand supports cloud consumption and paid
AI services, investment can be justified. If monetisation is delayed, the same spending becomes a
valuation and margin risk.

Key terms
Policy rate: the central bank's short-term interest-rate setting. Term premium: compensation for
holding long-dated bonds. Real yield: bond yield after expected inflation. Duration: sensitivity of
an asset's value to changes in discount rates.
