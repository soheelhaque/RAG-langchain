SYNTHETIC FINANCIAL RESEARCH NOTE
Title: Macroeconomic Conditions and Cross-Asset Risk Factors
Date: 2026-06-30
Source: Northstar Research, fictional training corpus

Growth backdrop
The fictional US economy expanded at a 1.8 percent annualised rate in the first quarter of 2026.
Consumer spending slowed from the prior year but remained positive, supported by real wage growth and
low unemployment. Business investment was split: spending on AI data centres and automation remained
strong, while office equipment and non-residential construction outside technology were subdued. This
two-speed pattern complicates broad market forecasts because aggregate GDP can look stable while many
industries experience weak demand.

Labour market and inflation
The unemployment rate was 4.3 percent and wage growth was 3.6 percent year over year. Labour-market
cooling was gradual rather than abrupt. Core services inflation remained near 3 percent, with shelter
and insurance costs offsetting lower goods prices. A slower return to the 2 percent target could keep
real yields and the term premium elevated. It also affects household purchasing power and the pricing
power of consumer-facing firms.

Fiscal and bond-market conditions
Government borrowing remained high, increasing the supply of long-dated bonds. Bond investors cited
fiscal uncertainty as one reason for a higher term premium in ten-year yields. A rise in the term
premium is different from a central-bank rate hike, but both can increase the discount rate used by
equity investors. Higher long-term yields pressure rate-sensitive sectors, including real estate,
utilities, small-cap growth, and richly valued technology companies.

Global and trade risks
Manufacturing surveys in major export markets were mixed. New restrictions on advanced semiconductor
equipment and a modest increase in import tariffs created uncertainty for globally exposed technology
and industrial companies. A stronger domestic currency would reduce the translated value of overseas
revenue. Semiconductor suppliers face concentrated customers, export-policy uncertainty, and the
possibility that a change in hyperscale capital expenditure reaches order books with a delay.

Credit and liquidity
Investment-grade credit spreads were near their five-year average, signalling that markets did not
expect an immediate recession. Lower-quality credit spreads were wider, especially for companies with
near-term refinancing needs. Private-credit lenders tightened underwriting standards for data-centre
projects without long-term power contracts or committed tenants. Liquidity conditions can change
quickly if inflation surprises raise bond yields and increase funding costs.

Macro scenarios
The base scenario is moderate growth, inflation easing gradually, and two policy-rate cuts over the
next twelve months. The upside scenario has faster disinflation and stronger productivity from
automation, which could lower yields while supporting earnings. The downside scenario combines sticky
inflation, higher term premium, and a softer consumer. Technology equities then face lower earnings
confidence and valuation compression. AI infrastructure demand would be more resilient than
discretionary software spending, but suppliers could be affected if cloud providers cut capital
expenditure plans.

Cross-document research prompt
To assess US technology equity risk, connect the inflation and yield outlook with cloud-provider
capital expenditure, evidence of AI monetisation, data-centre power availability, and 2027 earnings
revisions. Risk rises when several signals weaken together.
